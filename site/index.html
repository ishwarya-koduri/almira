A private family record

Everything your family owns and owes, in one calm, private place.

So nothing is forgotten while you are here, and no one is left guessing when you are not.

Why it is called Almira

Most Indian homes have an almirah: the steel cupboard with the locker inside, where the gold is kept, and the fixed deposit receipts, the LIC bonds, the property papers and the passbooks. Everyone in the house knows it is there. Very few people know everything that is in it.

Almira is the record of that cupboard, and of what never fitted in it: the mutual funds, the provident fund, the chit, the plot in the village, the loan your brother still owes, the home loan you owe. You write it down once. Your family can find it when it matters.

It never moves money and never asks for a bank password. It is a register, not a bank.

Three locks, one inside the other

Joining a family on Almira does not mean giving up your privacy inside it. Each lock below is enforced by the system, not by a promise.

Each record has its own door

You decide who in the family sees each holding. No one else can read your private records, not even the person who set up the family. The database itself applies the rule, on every read.

Numbers are locked when they are stored

Account numbers, policy numbers and your documents are encrypted with a key kept outside the database. By default only the last four digits of a number are kept at all.

Some things only you can open

Seal any field with a passphrase that never leaves your device. Almira stores something it cannot read, and cannot recover it for you if the passphrase is lost.

What these locks do not protect against is written down too, on the security page.

What we never do

  • We never move your money.Almira cannot pay, transfer, buy or sell anything.
  • We never ask for a bank password.Not to import, not to verify, not ever.
  • We never hold your money.There is no wallet and no balance with us. Your money stays where it is.

In your language

Almira is being written in English, Telugu and Hindi. Home, Settings and the family screen are translated today. The rest is still in English, and we would rather tell you that here than have you find it out.

Amounts are written the Indian way in every language: ₹1,76,875, never ₹176,875. The grouping is how the amount is said here, not an English habit.

₹1,76,875

One lakh, seventy-six thousand, eight hundred and seventy-five rupees. An example, not anyone's balance.
